Abstract. Breast thermographies can show temperature distribution
 and detect abnormalities in the body on examination.
This work shows
 an implementation allowing their used for three-dimensional (3d) models
 of the breast surface from their acquisition in five different angles.
Firstly,
 breast segmentation is performed using U-Nets trained for each angle.
 Then, the segmented edges are transformed into parametric curves.
Geo-
 metric transformation converts these curves from two-dimensional (2d)
 to a three-dimensional after proper rotations and translation of them.
These 3D curves are described by B-splines curves and used to build a
 Rational Non-Uniform B-splines Surface (NURBS) for the breast region.
Texture mapping is applied to project the frontal thermal data onto the
 3d surface.
A Likert scale-based survey was conducted with healthcare
 experts, and experienced academics to evaluate the final results and com-
 pare them with examples of the same persons.
The results received high
 grades of approval and agreements.
Keywords: Thermal imaging Breast cancer Three-dimensional
 models
1 Introduction
Breast cancer is the most common type of cancer among women both in Brazil
 and globally.
As reported by the National Cancer Institute 73,610 new cases of
 breast cancer were diagnosed in the country last year [1].
Projections indicate
 that by 2040, this number could increase to over 3 million of new cases [2].
There are many exams to detect breast cancer, mammography is the most
 popular.
Despite, the accumulative effect of the ionizing radiation of this exam,
 mammography is considered the gold standard [3].
Thermography is a non-
 invasive exam that analyses the heat emission from the body (breast region in
 this case).
It is affordable, fast, can be used in young and pregnant women, and

A abnor-

 because it can increase the metabolism of the area [7].
There are various types of treatments for breast cancer, which can be
 employed individually or in combination, depending on the stage of the dis-

Currently, mastectomy

After surgery to treat breast cancer, breast reconstruction may be

This reconstruction can be done through implants
 (prosthetic reconstruction) or through the transfer of tissue from other parts of
 the body (autologous reconstruction) [8].
The use of three-dimensional models can bring significant benefits to the

 for patients related to the shape similarity.
The process of creating these 3D
 models can be carried out through the utilization of a scanner, which captures
 detailed information about the three-dimensional coordinates of the breast [9].
However, it is important to mention some disadvantages associated with this
 approach, such as the high costs involved and the lack of access to this technology
 in hospitals and clinics, as well as the potential inaccuracy in cases of considerable
 breast size.
2 Materials and Methods
This study uses thermal imaging from the database published on the Visual Lab
 website [10, 11].
The $640\times480$ images were obtained with a FLIR SC620 (sensi-
 tivity of $0.04^{\circ}C$ and range from $-40^{\circ}C$ to $500^{\circ}C$).
This database contains 222
 exams, but there are exams unsuitable for the purpose of this work (e.g. those
 with capture on incorrect angles, mastectomized patients and unclear breast
 edges), they were removed.
After this, 140 exams could be used, each one pre-
 senting breast images acquired in 5 direction: frontal $(0^{\circ})$, oblique right lateral
 $(45^{\circ})$ total right lateral $(90^{\circ})$, oblique left lateral $(-45^{\circ})$ and total left lateral
 $(-90^{\circ})$.
The temperature matrices from these exams are used. The developed
 approach employs techniques from two areas of computer vision (CV): image
 processing (IP) and computer graphics (CG).
IP methods are used at the begin-
 ning to obtain aspects of the geometry of the breasts from the exams, and in the
 final step, to map textures corresponding to the temperatures.
The intermediate
 part of the process uses CG methods (or vector imaging) for construction and
 positioning of curves, definition of transformation matrices and generation of 3-d
 curves and the surface representing the breast under examination.

Preprocessing takes the original images $(640\times480)$ and resized it to $256\times256$
 pixels.
The frontal exam is converted from the original format to RGB (Red,
 Green, Blue) using the FLIR (Forward Looking Infra-Red) rain palette.
In this
 way, the frontal information is used in the final step for rendering the 3d surface
 by using texture mapping.
Segmentation uses U-net model of Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN) to
 performed the breast segmentation [12,13].
In fact, 5 CNNs of this model are
 trained (i.e. hyper parameters are generated for 5 U-nets) each to be used in one
 of the 5 exam positions.
From the database subset used, 109 (78%) exams were
 used for training the nets and 31 (22%) were separated for testing.
The training
 used 100 epochs in batches of 2 with 50 epochs.
Net weights are initialized with
 normal distribution of random values.
Each convolution uses the rectified linear
 unit activator (ReLU) function to allow passing values that are greater than
 0. Adam optimizer with learning rate $1\times10^{-4}$ is used [14].
This allows the
 region of the breasts to be separated (i.e. segmented) from the rest of the image,
 as shown in Fig. 2. It is noteworthy to emphasize that, given the preliminary
 nature of this study, segmentation evaluations in comparison with a ground truth
 standard were not conducted.
The analysis of segmented images was subjectively
 carried out for validation purposes.
Those results without apparently satisfactory
 segmentations were simply excluded of the process.

Identification of the relevant edge and points of each view are important
 because they help in identifying the shape of the breasts, both in width (front)
 and in thickness and height (sides).
They will be used later to generate the 3D
 surface.
The inframammary curves are of main interest, these will be used to
 adjust all other curves from the relevant edges of each view of the exam in a
 single 3D coordinate system.
To obtain these elements, the identification of the
 relevant edges uses 4 steps.
(1) Initially, the points in the lower region of each
 edge are found, looking for the first points from bottom to top in each result of
 the previous step.
That is, for each of the final pixels of the horizontal direction,
 the first pixel of the edge is searched from bottom to top.
This will be stored and
 the subsequent ones ignored. Thus, at the end of this step, only the pixels closest
 to the bottom of each of the 5 edges from the previous step will be available,
 as shown in Fig. 4(a).
(2) Then, a search is made for the first pixels vertically,
 starting from the right side of each image.
Unlike the bottom-up search, the
 lateral search consists of a lateral scan of each of the final edge pixels starting
 at the right end (to find the closest pixels on this side).
Output of this step is
 shown in Fig. 4(b). (3) Same idea is applied to define the edge on the left (to find
 the closest pixels on this side), this result is shown in Fig. 4(c).

To describe the 3D shape of the breast, 9 curves are used (see Fig. 7. From a
 $0^{\circ}$ image, three curves are generated following different criteria: the first curve
 is defined by all points to the right of P1; the second by all points to the left of
 P3; and the third is an auxiliary curve that extends from P2 to the top of the
 image (Fig. 5). Then, four curves represent the complete edges of images rotated
 at $45^{\circ}$, $-90^{\circ}$, $-45^{\circ}$, and $90^{\circ}$, respectively. Additionally, two auxiliary curves are
 created by replicating the edges of $45^{\circ}$ and $-45^{\circ}$, which 
will later be rotated
 at angles of $135^{\circ}$ and $-135^{\circ}$, respectively (forming the central valley between
 beasts).
Geometric transformations of the key points needs to use a 3D coordinate
 system (Fig. 7).
The X and Y axis of this coordinate is the same horizontal and
 vertical directions of the 2d images, respectively, while the Z axis is defined by
 the vector product of the X by Y. The edge of the $0^{\circ}$ image is not rotated.
This edge will serve as the base of the 3d model in the z-plane.
The edges
 from the $45^{\circ}$ and $-90^{\circ}$ breast views will have their contours rotated by $45^{\circ}$
 and $-90^{\circ}$, respectively, and positioned over point P3 obtained in the $0^{\circ}$ view.
The edges from the $-45^{\circ}$ and $90^{\circ}$ breast views will have their edges rotated
 by $-45^{\circ}$ and $90^{\circ}$, respectively, and positioned over point P1 obtained in the $0^{\circ}$
 view.
The two auxiliary contours created from copying the $45^{\circ}$ and $-45^{\circ}$ edges
 will be rotated by $135^{\circ}$ and $-135^{\circ}$.
The processes of translation and rotation
 are performed through transformation matrices, and all the points of the curves

4 Results
This article presents an system to model the body from 5 thermograms and
 also a way to produce a 3D visualization for thermographic examinations.
The
 methodology created in original and uses a combination of IP and CG techniques.
The first part of this methodology extracts the shape of the breasts based on
 their edges and then the second part transform these edges in a 3d curves that
 generates a spatial model using NURBS.
The algorithm used in the system after
 the segmentation is present, as well.
140 exams was used generating the same
 numbers of 3D models and exams.
The generated models can be viewed at [19].
 Figure 9 shows the result for the volunteer with ID 143 of the data set bellow
 the original acquisitions for visual comparison.
Fig. 9. Thermographic images of volunteer ID 143 and she 3D reconstruction by this
 technique.
The 3D resultant models were visually evaluated by comparing the exams
 and the results in a survey using a five-point Likert scale.
Fifteen experts in the
 fields of healthcare, computer graphics, 3D modeling, and image processing, all
 with experience in medical image analysis, were consulted regarding 10 of the
 created 3D models.
The results achieved an approval rate of over 70%, which is
 considered a very good outcome [20].
In the complete implementation, 5 U-nets Convolutional Neural Networks
 (CNNs) were employed for the segmentation of breast regions.
As a future
 work, considering that the right and left lateral images are mirrored images,
 the segmentation process can be optimized by vertically flipping the images,
 thus training a single network to handle both lateral and oblique views.
This
 approach reduces the complexity of the model and simplifies the breast segmen-
 tation process.
Another enhancement would be the use of only one network to

In future works, we are considering also to compare
 the generated models with real 3-d models acquired through a 3D scanner and
